# Medieval II: Total War Academy — beginner guide

This guide combines mechanics documented in the manual with original tactical recommendations. Mods may alter the rules. It is not a reading of your active save.

## First turn — checklist

1. Check your florin balance
2. Review public order
3. Queue construction in key towns
4. Pick one economic center
5. Send a diplomat for trade rights
6. Scout the nearest target
7. Assemble infantry and cavalry
8. Secure border and garrison
9. Save before a major war
10. Review taxes and queues before ending turn

## Your first 20 turns

### Turns 1–3
Check balance, start a useful building, seek trade rights and scout your first target.

### Turns 4–8
Build one balanced field army, protect borders and keep recently captured towns stable.

### Turns 9–15
Develop a few trade and recruitment hubs. Avoid multiple simultaneous wars without reserves.

### Turns 16–20
Reassess treasury trend, upkeep, diplomacy and your next expansion direction.


## Your first turn without panic

What to do before you press End Turn.

1. Open your faction overview: check treasury, projected balance, troops and missions.
2. Open every city and castle. Check public order and whether construction has started.
3. Do not recruit everywhere at once: most troops cost upkeep each turn.
4. Send a diplomat to a safe neighbor with a trade-rights proposal.
5. Choose ONE realistic expansion target. Check your army and reinforcements.
6. Before ending the turn, review idle troops, queues, taxes and vulnerable borders.

**Watch out:** Do not spend your whole treasury on troops on turn one.


## Economy and making florins

Earn more than your armies cost.

1. Check your balance, not just treasury: 20,000 florins with a 3,000-per-turn loss disappears quickly.
2. In cities, consider markets, roads, ports and farms; actual returns depend on the province.
3. Roads improve trade and movement for armies and agents.
4. Ports enable sea trade, but require coastal access.
5. Mines help where the province has suitable deposits; look for gold and silver.
6. Increase taxes only while public order remains safe.
7. Sign trade rights and avoid maintaining excess garrisons without a purpose.

**Watch out:** A resource’s trade_value is NOT direct income per turn.


## What to build in cities and castles

Economic versus military priorities depend on the settlement’s role.

1. Safe rear city: consider trade, roads, farms and public order.
2. Coastal city: consider a port when sea trade can function.
3. Frontier city: secure defenses and reserve money for a garrison.
4. Castle: invest in the troop types you actually plan to recruit.
5. Before building, compare price, construction time and tooltip benefits.
6. Economic buildings do not yield identical returns in every province.

**Watch out:** Do not build expensive barracks everywhere; use a few specialist recruitment centers.


## Public order and settlement growth

How to avoid losing a newly conquered city.

1. After conquest, check public order, religion, taxes and damaged buildings.
2. With low public order, lower taxes and keep a garrison before advancing.
3. A governor with suitable traits can improve settlement conditions.
4. Priests help convert a province with a different religion.
5. Distinguish population-and-trade-focused cities from better-defended castles.

**Watch out:** High taxes boost revenue but reduce public order.


## Building and recruiting an army

Unit cooperation matters more than one big stat.

1. Build an infantry line that can hold through missile fire and charges.
2. Add missile units to weaken enemies before contact.
3. Keep cavalry on the flanks; exploit room and charge timing.
4. Use spearmen or pikemen to protect against enemy horsemen where appropriate.
5. Check morale, armor, armor-piercing, recruitment cost and upkeep.
6. Retrain and upgrade equipment when the required buildings are present.

**Watch out:** Avoid building an entire army around one unit type with no counterplay plan.


## Offense: how to win battles

A simple field-battle plan.

1. Inspect terrain before engagement: hills, woods, rivers and obstacles.
2. Do not sprint the whole army immediately. Keep formation and watch fatigue.
3. Pin the enemy with infantry from the front, then strike its flank or rear with cavalry.
4. Pull cavalry away after contact when feasible and set up another charge.
5. Missile troops work better with a clear line of sight and fewer friendlies blocking shots.
6. When enemies rout, avoid putting your general at risk in a lone pursuit.

**Watch out:** Do not charge heavy cavalry head-on into a prepared spear wall.


## Defense, ambushes and sieges

Position and morale can matter more than numbers.

1. Hills can help you maintain a line and control enemy approaches.
2. Screen missile troops with infantry; do not expose them to cavalry.
3. During wall defense, keep reserves near likely breaches.
4. For an assault, bring ladders, rams or siege equipment and consider multiple approaches.
5. Watch for routs and the resulting morale collapse.

**Watch out:** Do not leave your entire army under tower and arrow fire without an assault plan.


## Diplomacy and alliances

How to choose allies and avoid too many enemies.

1. Start with trade rights from factions you do not intend to conquer immediately.
2. An alliance can secure a quiet border, but never assume the AI will always honor it.
3. Avoid opening several wars at once; armies and money are limited.
4. Monitor neighbors and religious conflicts; Catholic factions must also mind papal relations.
5. Treat military access separately from alliance: not every friendly faction will open its borders.
6. Reassess alliances every few turns as wars and borders change.

**Watch out:** Do not assume a specific faction is always an ideal ally without current border and war data.


## Agents, priests, spies and merchants

Small characters, big campaign effects.

1. Diplomats unlock trade, alliances and negotiations — send them to neutral factions.
2. A merchant standing on a resource may generate additional income.
3. Merchant income depends on finance skill, distance, resource and trade rights, among other things.
4. A priest helps religious conversion and stability in difficult regions.
5. A spy can scout garrisons; avoid blindly assaulting defended walls.
6. Assassins are risky; assess chances and expect possible failure.

**Watch out:** Rival merchants can attempt to acquire your trade assets.


## Religion, the Pope and rebellions

Keep conquests from ending in revolt.

1. After conquering another faith’s city, monitor religion and public order.
2. Consider a religious building and priest if locals follow a different faith.
3. Catholic rulers should watch for excommunication and papal demands.
4. Do not leave too many unstable settlements without garrisons.

**Watch out:** Holy wars and crusades depend on religion, events and the particular mod.


## Fleets, ports and transport

Naval warfare and trade links.

1. Ports improve sea trade and allow naval construction in coastal regions.
2. Protect trade routes; naval blockades may reduce income.
3. Do not send an important transport army without checking enemy fleets.
4. When reading EDU naval rankings, compare roles rather than one stat alone.

**Watch out:** A strong ship in EDU might be available late — check recruitment requirements.


## Cheats and safe trainer use

Learning aid without pretending to modify live memory.

1. In Kruchyx, open Trainer & MAX packs and select an action.
2. Add money: enter an amount, and Kruchyx prepares add_money commands respecting the per-call limit.
3. Select the character before using “this” and verify the exact trait identifier.
4. Paste commands into the (~) console and verify the effect. Not all codes work identically in mods.
5. Make a backup save before experimenting with new cheats.
6. Unlimited movement ON/OFF is not yet connected to game memory — its control stays disabled.

**Watch out:** Do not confuse a command generator with a live process-editing trainer.


## Mods, bugs and opportunities

How to use Kruchyx without damaging a campaign.

1. Select the correct mod folder first. Review the files Kruchyx has found.
2. Unit strength in EDU does not automatically tell you when troops can be recruited.
3. A potentially ungarrisoned rebel settlement is a lead, not proof of a free conquest.
4. Compare faction potential with campaign, building and resource data.
5. File inconsistencies are technical issues; a weak garrison is usually a tactical opportunity.

**Watch out:** File analysis does not reveal the current state of a saved campaign.

## Kruchyx Academy — mechanics and exploits (v0.9.2)

**Single-player experiments only. Make a separate save first.** Open **Secrets & exploits** in the app for step-by-step instructions, reliability labels and source links.

### Merchant stacking on a resource

1. Find a valuable resource and record the income of one merchant.
2. Put your own army — preferably with a general and a cheap unit — on the resource.
3. Try merging several merchants into that army.
4. End a turn and compare real income to the previous save.
5. Test whether rival merchants can acquire protected merchants.

Do not assume income scales linearly. Players report diminishing returns and risks of rebellion: https://steamcommunity.com/app/4700/discussions/0/5962175849943438686/

### Extreme-duration diplomacy tribute

1. Save separately and meet a faction with your diplomat.
2. Make sure you **demand** tribute **from the AI**, rather than offer to pay it yourself.
3. Experiment with an extreme duration (for example 99,999,999 turns, if accepted by the UI).
4. Test a large amount such as 1,000,000 florins per turn.
5. Inspect the deal valuation and check whether it incorrectly becomes very generous.
6. An **unverified, user-supplied variation** adds a one-time gift of 3,333,333 florins. It is NOT known to be necessary or universally effective.
7. If accepted, end one turn and verify actual treasury receipts.

Community discussions document the general valuation glitch, not guaranteed success for the exact amounts: https://www.reddit.com/r/Medieval2TotalWar/comments/1e975o3/exploit_guide/ and https://steamcommunity.com/app/4700/discussions/0/592888463635776813/
